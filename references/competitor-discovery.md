# Competitor discovery · 观

1. Define user task, search intent, target market, device, constraints.
2. Select 3–6 relevant competitors or exemplars, spanning direct rivals and best-in-class specialists; fewer if the niche is thin.
3. For each dimension (design, UX, features, information architecture, SEO, performance/mobile, reliability/accessibility), identify the strongest **observed** reference, not an assumed universal champion.
4. Prefer live pages and real task flows; collect canonical URLs, observation date, viewport, language, screenshots, and measurable observations. Respect authentication, access restrictions and robots/terms. Do not invent analytics, competitors, prices, rankings, traffic, or screenshots.
5. Inspect your own product with identical tasks and conditions. Treat comparisons without equivalent conditions as qualitative.

Separate evidence levels:
- E1: direct observed workflow, reproducible measurement or code/test.
- E2: documented official product behavior or credible independent report.
- E3: unverified assumption or marketing claim. Requires verification.

Deliver: competitors by role, comparison scenarios, strengths/weaknesses, evidence, uncertainty. Search rankings vary by region/time: do not claim competitors are ranked above us unless supported by current SERP/GSC evidence.
